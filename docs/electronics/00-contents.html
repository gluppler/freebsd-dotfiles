Reading the figures

Current is drawn as conventional current: positive charge leaving the + terminal of the source and returning to its − terminal. In metal wires the electrons actually drift the other way; every law here works the same either way as long as one direction is used throughout.

A voltage is always between two points. A + and − beside a part mark the reference direction: v is the potential at + minus the potential at −. With the current arrow entering the + terminal (the passive sign convention) the power p=vi is the power the part absorbs; a negative result means the part delivers power, as a source does.

Ground is the node every other voltage is measured from, 0 V by definition. A rail symbol such as +3V3 names a supply node; every symbol with that name is the same wire.

Reading with the keyboard

  • j and k scroll; gg and G go to the top and bottom.
  • f labels every link and control; type a label to follow it. H and L go back and forward.
  • / searches the page; n and N step through matches.
  • +, - and = zoom in, out and back; the figures stay sharp.
  • Pause motion at the top of every page freezes every figure where it is. With reduced motion preferred, the figures stay on their first frame.
