Reading a schematic

A schematic shows what connects to what, not where parts sit on the board. Each line is a conductor of no resistance; everything joined by lines is one node at one voltage. Parts carry a reference designator (R1, C3, U2) that matches the board and the parts list, and a value.

Signals usually flow left to right and higher voltages sit at the top, so supply rails run along the top and ground along the bottom.

Resistors, capacitors and inductors

Resistor · R

Sets how much current a voltage drives and turns that energy into heat. The current is proportional to the voltage across it.

v=iRp=i2R

Potentiometer · RV

A resistor with a sliding contact, the wiper. Its voltage moves between the two ends as you turn it: knobs, joysticks, servo position sensing.

V2=xV13 for travel x from 0 to 1, unloaded

Trimmer or rheostat · RV

A resistance you adjust, usually once, to calibrate a circuit.

Thermistor · RT

Its resistance falls as it warms (NTC). The β model fits most parts, with T in kelvin:

R=R0eβ(1/T−1/T0)

Photoresistor · R

Resistance falls with more light. Slow, tens of milliseconds, but simple light sensing.

Capacitor · C

Two plates and an insulator. It stores charge, passes changes in voltage and blocks a steady DC voltage once charged.

q=Cvi=Cdvdt

Electrolytic capacitor · C

A large value in a small part, but polarized: + must stay the more positive lead, or it fails, sometimes violently.

Inductor · L

A coil. It stores energy in its magnetic field and resists any change in its current.

v=Ldidt

Cored inductor · L

The lines mark an iron or ferrite core: more inductance in less space, until the core saturates and the inductance collapses.

Transformer · T

Two coils on one core. AC in one induces AC in the other; dots mark the ends that rise together. Ideal:

v2v1=N2N1=i1i2

Crystal · Y

A quartz resonator. With an amplifier it oscillates at one precise frequency: 32.768 kHz for clocks, 8 to 40 MHz for processors.

Fuse · F

A conductor that melts and opens the circuit when the current stays above its rating.

Ferrite bead · FB

A lossy inductor: it passes DC and turns high-frequency noise on a supply line into heat.

Sources

Voltage source · V

Holds its voltage VS whatever current flows; + marks the higher terminal.

v=VS

Cell and battery · BT

The long plate is +. An alkaline cell gives 1.5 V; a lithium-ion cell 3.6 to 3.7 V nominal, 4.2 V full.

AC source · V

A sinusoid of peak Vp and frequency f, with ω=2πf:

v(t)=Vpsin(ωt)

Current source · I

Drives current IS in the arrow's direction whatever the voltage across it.

i=IS

Controlled voltage source

The diamond means its value follows another voltage or current in the circuit. These model amplifiers:

v=Avx or v=rix

Controlled current source

A current set by another quantity; the model of a transistor:

i=gmvx or i=βix

Switches, relays and loads

Switch · SW

Open: no current. Closed: a connection of almost no resistance.

Push button · SW

Momentary and normally open: it connects only while pressed. Contacts bounce for a few milliseconds, which logic must ignore.

Relay · K

Current in the coil pulls the contact over. A small signal switches a separate, larger circuit; the coil needs a diode across it when switched off.

Motor · M

A DC motor's speed rises with voltage and its torque with current.

τ=ktie=keω

Lamp · DS

An incandescent indicator or bulb; its cold resistance is several times lower than hot.

Meters

A voltmeter goes across a part and draws almost no current; an ammeter goes in series, in the current path, and drops almost no voltage.

Diodes and transistors

Diode · D

Conducts from anode to cathode, the way the arrow points, once the anode is about 0.6 to 0.7 V higher (silicon); blocks the other way. The bar is the cathode, banded on the part.

Zener diode · D

Conducts backwards at its Zener voltage VZ and holds it there: a simple reference.

Schottky diode · D

Lower forward drop, 0.15 to 0.45 V, and fast: power paths and protection.

LED · D

A diode that emits light, dropping 1.8 V (red) to 3 V (blue, white). Its current must be limited, usually with a resistor:

R=VS−VFIF

Photodiode · D

Light makes a reverse current proportional to its brightness: fast light sensing.

NPN transistor · Q

A small base current lets a current β times larger flow from collector to emitter. The arrow on the emitter points out.

iC=βiBvBE≈0.7V

PNP transistor · Q

The mirror image: pulling the base below the emitter turns it on, and current flows from emitter to collector. The arrow points in.

N-channel MOSFET · Q

The gate is insulated. A gate-to-source voltage above the threshold Vth opens a channel from drain to source; the gate draws no steady current. Switches loads to ground.

P-channel MOSFET · Q

On when the gate is pulled below the source by more than |Vth|. Switches loads from the supply.

Amplifiers, logic and chips

Op-amp · U

A differential amplifier of enormous gain A, around 105 or more. Feedback from the output to the − input sets the useful gain.

vout=A(v+−v−)

D flip-flop · U

Copies D to Q at each rising clock edge and holds it in between: one bit of memory. The wedge marks the clock input.

Integrated circuit · U

A box with named, numbered pins grouped by function rather than by package position. Pin 1 is marked on the package by a dot or notch.

Logic gates have two drawing styles. The ANSI shapes are the common ones; IEC boxes carry a symbol for the function. A small circle on an output, or input, means inversion. The truth table lists the output for inputs AB = 00, 01, 10, 11 (NOT: A = 0, 1).

Grounds, rails and connectors

Ground · GND

The 0 V reference every voltage is measured from. Chassis is the metal case; the triangle often marks a separate signal or analog ground.

Rails and net labels

Every rail symbol or net label with the same name is the same wire, so a schematic need not draw every connection.

Junctions

A dot joins wires; crossing lines without one do not connect. Avoid four-way junctions, where a missing dot is easy to miss.

Connector · J

Pins that leave the board: headers, USB, battery and sensor leads. Pin 1 is marked on the footprint.

Test point · TP

A pad or loop for a probe or a test fixture.

Reading part values

Through-hole resistors carry colour bands: two or three digits, a multiplier, and a tolerance band set a little apart.

Brown, black, orange, gold reads 1, 0, ×1000: 10 kΩ ±5%. Five bands give three digits: brown, black, black, red, brown is 100 × 100 = 10 kΩ ±1%.

Surface-mount parts print digits instead. Three digits are two figures and a power of ten, so a resistor marked 103 is 10 × 10³ = 10 kΩ; four digits are three figures and a power, so 1002 is also 10 kΩ. R marks the decimal point: 4R7 is 4.7 Ω. A 0 or 000 resistor is a jumper. Capacitor codes count picofarads: 104 is 10 × 10⁴ pF = 100 nF; 225 is 2.2 µF.

Values come from the E series, spaced evenly on a logarithmic scale so that each step is about one tolerance apart. Every decade repeats them: 4.7 Ω, 47 Ω, 470 Ω, 4.7 kΩ.
